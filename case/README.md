# Case: print, assemble, regenerate

Case draft 4 (2026-11-26). Modelled only: nothing has been printed or test-fitted yet.

**Known mismatch:** the case model, the renders and the assembly video were built with the earlier battery block (two variants A/B, bosses on the mounting face, right block not screwed down). The board now carries one common block at all three cells. The case has to be rebuilt once the block's mounting holes are final.

## Hardware

The full list with quantities, part numbers and notes is the **Mechanical** sheet of the schematic (`mechanical.kicad_sch`). Export it with:

```
kicad-cli sch export bom FCL.kicad_sch --fields "Reference,Value,Category,Qty,Manufacturer,MPN,Supplier_Link,Material,Source_File,Notes" -o mech_bom.csv
```

In short:
- 8 x M2.5 heat-set inserts (4 case corners in the rear shell, 4 camera posts in the front shell)
- 4 x M2.5 x 10 socket head screws (case corners)
- 4 x M2.5 x 6 socket head screws (camera module to the front shell)
- 1 x 1/4-20 heat-set insert (tripod, bottom face of the rear shell)
- Battery blocks: M2.5 x 6 screws, small M2.5 washers as 0.5 mm spacers, 2 mm dowel pins
- Per cell: MS3181-16 cap, O-ring 20 x 1, three bayonet pins, brass contact disc, Keystone 5220 spring, brass slug

## Assembly

`renders/FCL_assembly.mp4` shows these steps.

1. **Blocks onto the PCB.** Screw the three aluminium end blocks on with M2.5 screws from the camera side, one small washer between block and PCB at each screw. Cells 1 and 3 have their block on the left edge, cell 2 on the right edge.
2. **PCB-side cell cradle** onto the back of the PCB.
3. **LCD-side cell cradle** on top of it. It also carries the LCD.
4. **LCD.** Push the ribbon into J7 and close the latch, then lay the TFT on the cradle. The LCD goes on before the case.
5. **Rear shell.** Press in the four M2.5 inserts and the 1/4-20 insert. Snap the two button plungers in from the inside, barbed end first, until they click into the counterbore on the eye face.
6. **Drop the PCB and LCD stack into the rear shell.**
7. **Camera into the front shell.** Four M2.5 x 6 screws into the inserts in the posts.
8. **Front shell on.** The camera header plugs into J1 as the shell closes.
9. **Four M2.5 x 10 case screws** in the corners.
10. **Cells.** Slide them in through the blocks: cells 1 and 3 from the left, cell 2 from the right.
11. **Caps.** Brass slug and spring go in after the cell, then the MS3181 cap: push on and turn a quarter.

The video shows the right-hand block only being seated at step 1; it is now screwed like the others.

Preparing a cap (once per cap): dot of RTV on the cap's gasket, press the brass disc onto it, solder the spring lug to the disc.

## Regenerating

The case follows the PCB, so it is generated by script (CadQuery) from a STEP export of the board.

```
kicad-cli pcb export step FCL.kicad_pcb -o board.step
FCL_BOARD_STEP=board.step python case/assembly/build_case.py        # 5 to 10 minutes
python case/assembly/render_case.py
python case/assembly/make_assembly_video.py
flatpak run --command=freecadcmd org.freecad.FreeCAD case/assembly/freecad_case.py case case/freecad/FCL_case.FCStd
```

Needs Python with CadQuery and VTK, plus ffmpeg for the video. `build_case.py` prints an interference check at the end; the only overlaps expected are the O-ring squeeze and the bayonet-pin press fit in the cap stacks.
